The fifteen countries

One walk from the wood floor to the frozen summit — twenty levels each, three hundred in all. Every palette below is the one the game already ships, so a painted background will sit inside its country rather than fight it.

What the art has to do

Shape. 7 : 8 portrait — the board renders at 343 × 392, so generate at 1400 × 1600 and it has room to spare on a retina screen.
  • Top third — the hero. Sky, canopy, waterfall crown, aurora. This band is never covered, so everything worth looking at belongs here.
  • Middle third — quiet. Letters land here. Soft, low-contrast, no fine texture and no focal point, or the words become unreadable the moment the board fills.
  • Bottom quarter — the floor. A grounded edge for the first row of tiles to stand on.
The one hard rule. Letter tiles are pale cream with dark brown type. Wherever a tile can land, the background must stay mid-tone or deeper — never cream, sand or pale gold, or the letters vanish into it. Keep the pale values up in the sky where nothing covers them.

Never include: creatures or characters (the friends are separate painted sprites that walk on top), any text or lettering, a strong centred subject, or high-frequency detail below the halfway line.

Style. Painterly storybook, soft edges, visible brush — the same hand as the creature portraits.

If you generate motion: slow and peripheral only — drifting cloud, falling snow, a shimmer on water, floating spores. Nothing fast, nothing high-contrast, nothing that crosses the middle band.
