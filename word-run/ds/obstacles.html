Obstacles: what the measurement actually found

255 obstacle-and-country pairs, sampled from real rendered pixels in all fifteen countries, measured two ways: WCAG contrast, which only sees lightness, and deltaE, which sees colour.

First, a correction to what I told you earlier. I said bramble at 1.26:1 and branch at 1.20:1 were "close to invisible" against the painting. That was contrast only, and contrast is blind to hue. Measured perceptually, bramble sits at deltaE 41 and branch at 46 against their backgrounds - both far above the threshold where two colours are hard to separate. They are low in lightness difference, not low in colour difference. They are not the problem. I have to fix that line in the handbook.
The real problem is somewhere else, and it is worse. Ice, mist and frost are painted in fixed colours that never change, while the letter tile underneath them takes the country's palette. In the pale, cold countries the letter tile drifts into the same colour as the coating and the two collide. 29 of 255 pairs fall under deltaE 12, and seven are under 5, which is the point at which two colours are the same colour. An iced letter you cannot identify is not an aesthetic problem: you will try to use it in a word, and it will not work.

Where it happens

29 pairs under deltaE 12, concentrated in five obstacles: ice 9, mist 8, stone 4, pest 4, frost 4. Every one of them is a trouble that sits on a letter rather than replacing it.

What I would do

A on its own. It is the only option that removes the cause rather than the symptom, it changes nothing a player can name, and it cannot drift again because the gap becomes a rule instead of a set of values somebody has to keep in step. B is worth adding later for low-vision players, since the lightness contrast is genuinely poor across the board, but it changes how the game looks and that is a separate decision from fixing a bug. D is a patch list and will rot the moment a sixteenth country appears.
